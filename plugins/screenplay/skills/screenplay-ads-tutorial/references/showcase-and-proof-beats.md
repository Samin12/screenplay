# Full showcase clips and big proof beats

In a tutorial about a build, the finished pieces are the stars. His ask: "Make sure the assets I gave you
are fully shown in each level." Later: "For Level 2, show this full video".

## Full showcase clips

A showcase clip is a finished piece played whole, full screen, with its own sound, as its own segment in
the assembly and its own scene in Borumi.

Where they go:
- **Cold open**: one finished ad before he speaks.
- **At the top of a level**: the piece that level will build (or, for a level that starts from footage, the
  source footage in full).
- **At the moment he gets it back**: right after the sentence that ends "…and it should come back" or
  "…a video like this", the finished piece plays in full. The lane does not render it; it reports the split
  time and the boss inserts the clip. See [assembly-and-checks.md](assembly-and-checks.md).

How each is built:
- 1920×1080, 30 fps, the piece untouched.
- **A chip** in a corner saying what it is: "Level 2 · The finished promo", or "WHAT CLAUDE SENT BACK".
- **A credit line**, small, for anything he did not make: "<Event> recap · made by the event".
- **Loudness about −17 LUFS**, a touch under his voice at −16. The first master had them at −19 and the
  review called them 3 dB too quiet; a piece copied from his project at −8 LUFS was clipping. Measure, then
  set one gain per clip.
- Its own chapter and chapter card when it is long enough to be a destination ("The finished promo").
- The first and last frames must be clean: the clip's own opening frame, sharp, and no half-built title
  after it.

## The real outputs, inside lanes

When he says "like this", "this is an example", "you can see", the real video or image is on screen, big and
playing. Not a poster still, not a thumbnail in a list.

- Before/after pairs sit side by side at full width, labelled. A tall stacked pair is re-stacked side by
  side for the full-screen layout.
- Figures come from the build's own logs (credits, minutes, shot counts), shown exactly when he says them.
  If the log disagrees with him, show the log's figure and tell the boss.
- A step that did not happen in the real build is not shown as if it did. If he says "this is an example of
  what it may look like", build it from real frames and label it ("Recreated from the cut list").

## Third-party footage

Other people's work, used as proof that something is possible:
- Credited on the frame: the source line with the domain and capture date.
- Small enough to read as a quotation, or in a browser frame under its address bar. Never full bleed as if
  it were his.
- Muted. Only the verifier's safe seconds.
- Never implies they used his method or his skill.
- A spec ad for a brand he does not work with is described as a spec ad in the handoff and never shown as
  if the brand ran it.

## Award and press beats: big

When he mentions a win, the proof is the footage of it and the press about it.

- Award clips fill the frame (full bleed). A press article fills at least 85% of the frame width, cropped
  to the headline and photo, with the source line on the card and the highlight on the words he says.
- Each beat lands on the words it shows. The clip of him holding the award goes on the title, not at the
  end.
- Hold long enough to read: about 2.5 s for an article, 3.5 s for a clip that needs watching. A 1.3 s beat
  that opens on a cropped headline was flagged.
- Check the first frame of each: a phone pan that starts with half the stage text cut off needs a later in
  point.
- Other people in press photos are public and press-named; still, do not add their names on screen.

## Checks before hand-off

- At 1/3 size, is the piece the biggest thing on screen?
- Does every third-party frame carry a source line?
- Does every full clip start and end on a finished frame?
- Loudness of each full clip measured and written in the handoff.
