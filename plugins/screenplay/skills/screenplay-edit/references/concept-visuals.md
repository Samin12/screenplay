## 1. What worked in his top videos

**Result first, then proof montage (hook).** 1.8M, 0:00-0:12: the first frame is the payoff, a line
chart "Copy McCaul's trades vs S&P 500" with stat cards *+15.5%* / *+34.8%* / *+$9,650, 2.2x the S&P*.
Then 1-3 s per beat, in the order he names the capabilities: dot cluster "Regular traders $500-$10K"
vs one big circle "$50M single trade, Whale" with the line "They didn't flip a coin. They know
something."; the Claude head logo dissolving in; the real Capitol Trades page; the Alpaca orders page
with yellow marker highlights; the Claude app saying "Both orders placed successfully". Face only
after the promise is shown (0:13). Why: the viewer sees the outcome and the real tools before any
claim, so the claim lands as proof.

**Level roadmap.** 1.8M 0:20-0:46 and 271K 0:24-0:45: blue numbered circles 1 2 3 pop on the grid,
the current one's title types on ("Setup", "Building a copy trading bot", "Options") with a small
preview card of that level's payoff that swaps every 2-4 s. Why: sets the ladder and teases the end.

**"Why this matters?" card.** Both trading videos: two words typed on the bare grid, 1-2 s, then the
stakes section. A clean beat that resets attention.

**One illustrated world for every story beat.** 1.8M 1:04-3:58 (also reused in 271K and 176K):
animated 16-bit pixel-art scenes in a curved CRT frame, each acting out his sentence literally:
JPMorgan trader at three monitors (1:04, "I worked at JP Morgan"); a poker table with our player's two
cards, opponent across, five face-down cards (1:18, "imagine a poker game"), and the cards **flip
face-up on "every card in your hand"** (1:24); a guy eating lunch, phone on the table showing a chart
(2:04, "you're at lunch, the window closes"); a trading floor team (2:24, "teams reading all that
information"); a 1950s broker on a rotary phone (2:58, "back in the day you'd call a person"); a phone
with a BUY button (3:14, "today it's an app"); the same desk with code on the monitor (3:24,
"connect through code"); a retro terminal "TRADING TERMINAL V1.0 · SIM MODE" (3:56, "paper
trading"); a man at a wall thermostat whose reading drops 70°F → 62°F and back (9:40). Each scene holds
5-9 s but something moves inside it on the punch word. Why: a story you can watch beats a label you
read, and one consistent world feels produced, not stock.

**Punchline line over dimmed B-roll.** 1.8M 1:08 and 1:42, 271K 2:00: the scene darkens and one
white line types on in the middle: "They see where the money is moving before you even hear about it
on the news." One line at the punch, never running subtitles.

## 2. Playbook

Visual language (all of it, every video): off-white paper with a light grey grid; the Claude
terracotta starburst as the hero/center; blue line icons with a soft glow and blue connector lines;
bold black sans labels (1-4 words); yellow = "the one we're on"; red = bad, green = good; stat cards in
pale beige/green. Story beats use the pixel-art CRT B-roll (section 3). Transitions: blur dissolve
between graphics, hard cut into B-roll. Everything lands on the word that names it.

| Concept | Recipe | Pacing | In screenplay |
|---|---|---|---|

## 3. B-roll with Higgsfield

The look is **not** photoreal stock footage. It is animated 16-bit pixel art with cinematic lighting,
framed like an old CRT: warm desk-lamp glow and a dusk city window (or cool blue for "Wall Street"),
one young protagonist (brown hair, dark suit or casual shirt) at desks with monitors showing charts or
an orange-accented Claude app, plants and a coffee mug, then barrel-curved edges, faint scanlines and
a vignette on black. Slow push-ins, small living motion, one reveal per clip.

Style block (keep it identical for every clip in a video):
```
STYLE="16-bit pixel art cutscene, detailed retro game style, clean crisp pixels, cinematic lighting with a warm desk-lamp glow and a dusk city skyline through the window, amber highlights and teal shadows, soft depth of field, slow camera push-in, subtle living motion, no on-screen text, no watermark."
```
Text-to-video (default):
```bash
higgsfield generate cost seedance_2_0 --prompt "$STYLE $SCENE $BEAT" --aspect_ratio 16:9 --duration 5 --resolution 720p --generate_audio false
higgsfield generate create seedance_2_0 --prompt "$STYLE $SCENE $BEAT" --aspect_ratio 16:9 --duration 5 \
  --resolution 720p --generate_audio false --wait --wait-timeout 20m     # prints the result URL
curl -L -o media/broll/<slug>.mp4 "<url>"
```
Same protagonist across clips, or a frame that must read exactly: make a keyframe first, then animate it.
```bash
higgsfield generate create nano_banana_pro --prompt "$STYLE $SCENE" --aspect_ratio 16:9 --resolution 2k \
  --image-references media/broll/hero.png --wait            # hero.png = one character sheet per video
higgsfield generate create seedance_2_0 --prompt "$STYLE Animate: $BEAT" --start-image media/broll/<slug>.png \
  --aspect_ratio 16:9 --duration 5 --resolution 720p --generate_audio false --wait
```
CRT frame (post, matches his frames; short GOP for the engine):
```bash
ffmpeg -i media/broll/<slug>.mp4 -vf "scale=1920:1080,lenscorrection=cx=0.5:cy=0.5:k1=0.10:k2=0.05,drawgrid=w=iw:h=4:t=1:c=black@0.18,vignette=PI/4.5,eq=saturation=1.08:contrast=1.04" \
  -an -c:v libx264 -crf 18 -g 30 media/broll/<slug>-crt.mp4
```
Notes: Seedance 2.0 makes audio by default, always pass `--generate_audio false`. Costs on Nov 6,
2026: Seedance 2.0 5 s 720p = 22.5 credits (1080p = 60), Nano Banana Pro 2k = 2. 720p is enough for
pixel art in a CRT frame. Use `--aspect_ratio 4:3` when the clip should fill the panel
(1400×990) instead of sitting in it as a framed 16:9 "TV". Write `$BEAT` as "At about 3 seconds,
<the reveal>" so the reveal can sit on the punch word. Keep keepers in a reusable library: the 271K
video reused the 1.8M poker and JPMorgan clips.

Example prompts (`$SCENE $BEAT`, used with `$STYLE`):
1. **Insider edge (poker).** "Over-the-shoulder view of a young man in a dark suit at a green felt poker table holding two face-down cards; across the table an older man in a blue shirt behind stacks of red, blue and white chips; five face-down cards in a row. At about 3 seconds the five table cards flip face up one by one and the opponent narrows his eyes."
2. **Wall Street sees it first.** "A dim blue trading floor, rows of glowing monitors, one trader in a suit watching a chart on his screen while the room around him is dark. At about 3 seconds the line on his chart spikes up and his screen glows green; he leans in."
3. **You miss the window.** "A young man in a green sweater eating lunch at a wooden diner table, his phone face up beside the plate showing a stock chart. At about 3 seconds the chart on the phone jumps and a BUY button flashes; he keeps eating and doesn't look."
4. **Back in the day.** "A 1950s brokerage office in warm sepia tones, a man in a brown suit at a desk piled with paper and a ticker-tape machine. At about 2 seconds he picks up a rotary phone and starts talking while the tape keeps printing."
5. **It runs while you relax.** "A young man leaning back in his office chair, hands behind his head, at a desk by a window with a dusk city skyline; on his monitor an app with an orange accent shows a flowchart of connected nodes. At about 3 seconds the nodes light up one by one and a notification bell rings on screen."
6. **Paper trading.** "Close-up of a chunky retro CRT computer terminal on a wooden desk, green text and a blinking cursor, a small paper sign on the desk. At about 2 seconds rows of buy and sell orders type onto the screen in green."
7. **Rules on autopilot (thermostat).** "A cozy living room at night, a man in a brown jacket facing a wall thermostat with a small glowing display. At about 2 seconds the display number drops, a heater clicks on, the room warms to orange, and the number climbs back up."
8. **Too much to manage.** "A cramped home office, one person surrounded by four monitors showing stock charts, crypto prices, currency rates and sports betting odds, sticky notes everywhere, a phone buzzing on the desk. At about 3 seconds more alerts pop up on every screen and he rubs his face with both hands."

## 4. Do / Don't

Do
- Open on the result, then show the real tools in the order he names them.
- Act every analogy out as a scene in one consistent illustrated world per video, with one reveal on the punch word.
- Follow a story with a 2-4 s clean diagram of the mechanic when there is one.
- Show every number he says, exactly as said, as a pair plus a translation (235×), with the source.
- Keep one canvas for a worked example and add a mark every 2-4 s; swap scenarios in place.
- Re-show the list skeleton (icon row, stacked pills) before each point, current item lit.
- Use callbacks: bring back the earlier analogy visual when he says "remember...".
- Put real numbers, names and UI in motion graphics or real screenshots, not in generated frames.
- Price B-roll first (`generate cost`), generate one clip per story beat, reuse keepers.

Don't
- Don't use generic photoreal stock-style footage; none of his top videos did.
- Don't air AI-generated text unchecked: the 399K video aired "Speadsheet", "Wack", "Connet Lgic" and "Triger". Prompt for no text and add labels in the edit.
- Don't hold a static slide or whiteboard: 25-35 s slides (375K) and the 97 s whiteboard hold (176K) are the slowest stretches in the set. Panel motion every ≤ 3 s (taste.md).
- Don't let a B-roll clip sit unchanged; if it has no reveal, cut it to 3 s or add the punchline line.
- Don't run captions as subtitles; one punchline line over a dimmed scene is the most text (METHOD.md: no burned-in captions).
- Don't mix illustration styles inside one stretch (photoreal 3D + sketch + doodle); pick one story world and one diagram language.
- Don't paraphrase with a visual: the poker cards flip on "every card in your hand", not on the sentence before.
